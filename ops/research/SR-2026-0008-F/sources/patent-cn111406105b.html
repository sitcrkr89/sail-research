[ Field of the invention ]
The present disclosure relates to methods and systems for culturing cells and harvesting biological agents. More specifically, the present disclosure relates to methods of cell culture by enhanced perfusion with continuous harvest without cell drainage.
[ Background of the invention ]
The demand for large amounts of therapeutic recombinant proteins has continued to increase since the beginning of biopharmaceutical production in the 1980 s. Developing a production process for producing recombinant proteins or other biological products is a complex task in which many variables must be balanced.
In a typical perfusion process, cells can be cultured for a long period of time by continuously replenishing the cells with fresh medium and draining the cells to maintain high cell viability. It is often necessary to periodically withdraw cells from the bioreactor during continuous production, which is relatively inefficient, as it can result in loss of cells and target biological products.
In a typical cell culture process, biological products secreted by cells are retained or harvested during cell culture, depending on the retention system used. In some cases, cells and biological products remain in the bioreactor during the culture process. For example, U.S. patent No. 9,469,865 discloses a perfusion process in which a cell culture comprising a biological substance and a cell culture is circulated over a separation system, wherein the biological substance is retained in or fed back to the reactor and the product is harvested at the end of the culture. The ultra-high solids content results in difficult clarification of the mixture of cells and biological products at harvest and ultra-low yields. In certain other cases, the cells and biological products are separated from the bioreactor during the culturing process.
There remains a need for improved cell culture processes to increase product yield, improve product quality and reduce costs. The present disclosure meets at least one of these needs by providing methods and systems for cell culture by enhanced perfusion with continuous perfusion without cell drainage.
[ Summary of the invention ]
The present disclosure relates to a method for producing biomass by perfusion culturing a cell culture in a bioreactor, wherein basal medium (basal medium) and feed medium (feed medium) are fed into the cell culture at different rates, and wherein the cell culture is passed through a separation system, and the biomass is continuously harvested. During the culturing process, the cells will remain in the bioreactor and will not be discharged. The method of the invention provides considerable advantages in terms of peak viable cell density and Qp (yield per cell). As a result, the present method can result in an improved productivity of the desired biological substance.
It has been found that by adding basal medium and feed medium to the cell culture at different rates, by varying the temperature during the culture, and by not draining the cell culture, a large amount of biomass can be achieved in the early stage and a high productivity in the later stage. Moreover, a coordinated separation system for continuous harvesting of biomass helps to achieve high Qp, better biomass quality and/or high purification yields. The methods of the present disclosure are referred to as enhanced perfusion culture (INTENSIFIED PERFUSION CULTURE, IPC) processes, where the perfusion process is coordinated with the continuous harvest process and cells are not drained during the culture.
In particular, the present disclosure provides a method for producing a biological substance, the method comprising: (a) Culturing a cell culture comprising a cell culture medium and cells; (b) Perfusing the cell culture with a basal medium and a feed medium in a bioreactor, and (c) harvesting the biomass, wherein the basal medium and the feed medium are fed into the cell culture at different rates, the cell culture continuously passing through a separation system, and the cells are retained in the bioreactor without being discharged throughout the culturing process.

"Cell culture" or "culture" refers to the growth and propagation of cells outside a multicellular organism or tissue. In principle, cells useful for producing a product are all cells known to the person skilled in the art that have the ability to produce a biological product. In this regard, a separation system having a hollow fiber filter is connected to the perfusion system. The hollow fiber filter of suitable pore size or molecular weight cut-off is selected such that the hollow fiber filter retains cells but not target products. When a cell culture solution containing a cell culture medium, cells (e.g., whole cells and lysed cells), a soluble expressed recombinant protein, a host cell protein, and waste, etc. is introduced into the filter, the hollow fiber membrane material can retain the cells in the inner diameter of the hollow fiber column of the filter and allow the target product, i.e., the soluble expressed recombinant protein, to be continuously harvested through the filter along with the culture medium. The retained cells are then returned to the bioreactor.
In various embodiments, any filter may be used as the separation system, provided that the appropriate pore size or molecular weight cut-off (MWCO) is selected to retain the cells but not the target product. In this example, the performance of the enhanced perfusion culture process (B) was directly compared with the performance of the conventional fed-batch culture process (a) and the concentrated fed-batch culture process (C) using clone X.
Traditional feed batch culture process A:
Process A was carried out in shake flasks. Conventional fed-batch culture process a was performed at an initial working volume of 50mL in a 250mL container volume. Cells were inoculated at 0.40X10 6 cells/mL in CDM4 medium (Hyclone) supplemented with 4.0mM L-glutamine and then cultured for 14 days. In the culture process, 3.00% of feed medium CB7a and 0.30% of feed medium CB7b were fed on days 3, 6, 8 and 10, respectively. The temperature was changed from 36.5 ℃ to 31.0 ℃ on day 5. The glucose concentration was maintained at 4.0g/L by adding 400g/kg of glucose stock solution throughout the culture process.
Reinforced perfusion culture process B:
Process B was performed in a 3L Applikon vessel using a delta V controller to control the temperature at 36.5℃and the pH in the range of about 7.2 to 6.8 and DO at 40% air saturation. A0.2 μm hollow fiber filter (Spectrum laboratories) operating in ATF flow mode was used for cell retention using the ATF-2H system (Refine Technology).
Culture was started at 0.80 to 1.00X 10 6 cells/mL in CDM4 medium (Hyclone) supplemented with 4.0mM L-glutamine. About 10 to 100ppm of defoamer was added daily from day 3. Basal medium (CDM 4, hyclone) was perfused from day 1, and the rate of 0.4VVD was increased to 1.5VVD on day 3. The perfusion of the feed medium (CB 7a/CB7 b) was initiated at a rate of 2.0% of the basal medium from day 4 and increased to 4.0% of the basal medium at day 7. Due to the decrease in cell density and cell viability, the perfusion rate of the feed medium gradually decreased from day 8 to 1% on day 17.
From day 3 to the end of the culture, the perfusion rate of CDM4 medium was maintained at 1.5VVD. The microbubble aeration device was used to deliver oxygen at a flow rate of 0.5 VVM. The temperature was changed from 36.5℃to 31.0℃on day 5 and maintained at 31.0℃until the end of the culture. Cell cultures were harvested continuously by ATF. Throughout the culture process, the cells remain in the bioreactor without being discharged.
Concentrated feed batch process C:
Process C was performed using a delta V controller to control the temperature at 36.5 ℃, the pH in the range between 7.2 and 6.8, and DO was set at 40% air saturation. The concentrated fed batch culture process was operated in accordance with process B, except that the pore size of the hollow fiber filters (Spectrum labs) was 50KD to retain both the cells and biological products in the culture broth.
